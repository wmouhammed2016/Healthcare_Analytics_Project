30-Day Readmission Trend (12 months)

All-cause, risk-adjusted. Monthly % of discharges readmitted within 30 days.
Insight: Readmissions dropped 18% YoY driven by improved discharge follow-up processes (post-discharge call program launched Oct 2024). However, Q3 (Jul–Sep) saw a mild uptick to 13.1% — directly correlated with the HAI spike in ICU, where sicker patients acquired infections that prolonged recovery and triggered returns. Targeted infection control in ICU should restore the downward trend by Q1 2026.

HAI Incidence by Pathogen

Hospital-acquired infections per 1,000 patient-days, by type, trailing 12 months.
Insight: C. difficile (34%) and catheter-associated UTIs (28%) account for 62% of all HAIs — both highly preventable. The CAUTI spike is concentrated in ICU where nurse-to-patient ratios slipped to 1:3 during Q3 peak. A return to 1:2 ratios plus mandatory catheter-removal review at shift change is projected to cut CAUTI by 60% within 90 days.

EDLOS Trend — Non-Admitted Patients (12 months)

Emergency Department Length of Stay from arrival to discharge, minutes.
Insight: EDLOS for non-admitted patients held under the 4-hour target through Q1–Q2, then climbed steadily from July onward — directly mirroring the burnout spike in the ED (see HR tab). The Q3 breach (avg 248 min vs 240 target) triggered ambulance diversion on 11 days. Adding 2 triage nurses per evening shift is projected to bring EDLOS back under target within 6 weeks.

Bed Occupancy by Day of Week

Average % of licensed beds occupied, by day, trailing 90 days.
Insight: Occupancy runs hot Tue–Thu (averaging 92%, above the 85% safe threshold) but drops sharply on weekends (Sat–Sun at 71%). The midweek strain drives boarding in the ER and pushes door-to-doctor times up. Discharge process improvements to push discharges earlier in the day (currently peaking at 4 PM) would smooth the curve and unlock ~12 midweek beds by 11 AM.

Revenue Cycle Funnel (Trailing 90 Days)

Volume and conversion at each of the 7 revenue cycle stages.
Insight: The funnel shows the biggest drop at Adjudication (68% → 89% pass-through), driven by a coding error spike following the EHR migration in May. The 6.8% denial rate translates to $1.94M in rework cost and 47-day A/R — direct cash-flow impact. Retraining 24 coders on the new EHR templates recovered 1.2pp in October, but full recovery to 5% target is projected for Q1 2026.

Claim Denial Reasons

Distribution of denied claims by root cause, trailing 90 days.
Insight: Coding errors (42%) and missing prior authorisations (24%) account for two-thirds of denials — both addressable through process and training fixes. Coding errors are concentrated in 3 DRG families (cardiothoracic, ortho, oncology) where the EHR migration changed default ICD-10 mapping. A targeted 2-day refresher for coders handling these DRGs is projected to cut coding-related denials by 50% within 60 days.

Burnout Prevalence by Unit (MBI High scorers)

% of staff scoring "High" on Maslach Burnout Inventory, by unit, latest survey.
Insight: ICU (38%) and ED (35%) are far above the 25% alert threshold — these are the same units driving the operational and clinical KPI degradation. The MBI sub-dimensions show "Emotional Exhaustion" is the dominant driver (not "Depersonalisation"), pointing to capacity strain rather than disengagement. This is reversible: hiring + ratio restoration typically reduces burnout by 8–12 pp within 6 months.

Nurse Turnover Trend (12 months)

Rolling 12-month nurse turnover %, all units vs ICU/ED.
Insight: Overall nurse turnover (19%) is creeping toward the national average (22%) but remains below it. However, ICU and ED turnover (28% and 31% respectively) is sharply worse — these two units lost 24 nurses combined in Q3, forcing 30% overtime coverage and creating the safety/quality ripple visible across all other pillars. Retention bonuses for ICU/ED nurses with 2+ years tenure are projected to halve turnover in those units within 12 months.

Inventory Turnover Trend (12 months)

Annualised turns, by month. Higher = inventory is moving faster.
Insight: Inventory turnover held within the 10–15x target band through Q2, then slipped in Q3 — the surge in critical-care demand (driven by the HAI spike in ICU) depleted safety stock faster than the replenishment cycle could respond. Turns recovered slightly in October as emergency orders arrived, but the underlying safety-stock parameters (set in 2022) need to be recalibrated to current demand patterns to prevent recurrence.

Stockouts by Category (Trailing 90 Days)

Number of stockout events per category, with patient-impact flags.
Insight: Pharmaceuticals (38% of stockouts) and surgical supplies (29%) dominate — and these are the categories with the highest patient-impact cost (cancelled procedures, delayed medication administration). 7 of the 12 stockout SKUs in the last 90 days were on the "critical" Class A list. Re-negotiating lead-time SLAs with the top 3 pharma suppliers and raising par levels on the top 20 critical SKUs is projected to cut stockouts by 70% within 60 days.

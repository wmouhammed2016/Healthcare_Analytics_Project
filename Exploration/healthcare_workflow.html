Before a patient steps foot in the hospital, a scheduling event creates the first data record. The appointment is booked — elective surgery, outpatient consultation, or a referral — and basic demographics are pre-verified against the insurer. This phase determines patient flow forecasting and OR block-time allocation.

FHIR R4 — Appointment Resource

Appointment.start, Appointment.end, Appointment.serviceType, Appointment.status (booked/cancelled). The encounter_id stub is created here as Encounter.id in planned status.

HIPAA — Safe Harbor (§164.514)

18 identifiers suppressed on first capture: patient_id replaces MRN; state-level geography only; birth_year replaces full DOB; age capped at 95.

CPT / HCPCS

Procedure type (cpt_code) is captured at scheduling to enable OR block allocation and surgical kit pre-staging by Supply Chain.

CMS — Prior Authorization

Elective procedures require pre-authorization from Medicare/Medicaid payers. Payer approval status is a financial gate before the encounter proceeds.

💸 Costs Incurred

→Scheduling staff labor cost (administrative FTE hours)
→EHR system licensing per transaction
→Prior authorization processing cost (~$11–$17 per request)

⚠️ Financial Risk

→No-show or late cancellation: OR block time wasted ($2,000–$5,000/hr)
→Payer denial of pre-auth → encounter deferred → revenue delayed
→Incorrect payer captured → claim denial downstream

FHIR R4 — Encounter Resource

Encounter.status transitions to "in-progress." Encounter.class (inpatient/outpatient), Encounter.period.start = admit_timestamp. Patient and Coverage resources linked.

HIPAA — PHI Protection

Full patient record created; ePHI protections apply. Security Rule requirements for access controls and audit logging activate. Breach Notification Rule also now in effect.

JCI 8th Ed — IPSG Goal 1

International Patient Safety Goal 1: Correct Patient Identification. Two patient identifiers (patient_id + birth_year) must be confirmed before any care is initiated. Wristband issued.

CMS — Conditions of Participation

Medicare/Medicaid CoPs require written informed consent, documentation of patient rights, and advance directive inquiry to be completed at admission.

HIMSS EMRAM Stage 3+

Computerized physician order entry (CPOE) and basic clinical documentation must be in place. ADT feed integration is a prerequisite for Stage 3 compliance.

💰 Revenue Initiated

→Facility fee charge begins accruing from admit_timestamp
→Patient co-pay / co-insurance collected at registration
→Per-diem inpatient bed charge starts (typically $1,500–$3,000/day)

💸 Costs Incurred

→Admissions staff labor (per-hour FTE)
→Bed preparation and housekeeping cost
→Real-time insurance eligibility verification fee (~$0.50–$2.00)
patient_responsibility_usd = allowed_amount_usd × deductible_rate + copay_amount

A triage nurse performs the first rapid clinical assessment. Vital signs are measured and recorded, acuity is scored using the Emergency Severity Index (ESI), and the severity_index is assigned — the single most consequential value in the dataset, as it propagates correlations into LOS, charges, OR time, and readmission risk. The door-to-doctor clock starts here.

Primary Clinical Act

Key Data Output

Cross-Domain Impact
severity_index drives LOS, OR time, submitted_charge, readmission_30d_flag

FHIR R4 — Observation Resource

Each vital sign is an Observation with Observation.code (LOINC), Observation.value, Observation.status, and Observation.category = "vital-signs."

LOINC Codes — Vital Signs

sbp_mmhg → LOINC 8480-6; spo2_pct → LOINC 59408-5; heart_rate_bpm → LOINC 8867-4; temperature_f → LOINC 8310-5; respiratory_rate → LOINC 9279-1.

JCI 8th Ed — IPSG Goal 6

Clinical risk assessment must be documented at admission. Triage severity scoring is the mechanism. JCI requires hospitals to define and use a validated triage system consistently.

ISO 7101 — PDSA Cycle Initiation

If a safety incident occurs in a high-acuity triage scenario, the PDSA cycle (pdsa_cycle_id) is initiated here. Plan phase = identifying the quality problem.

HIMSS EMRAM Stage 6+

Vitals must be captured electronically and flow into the clinical decision support system. Manual vital sign recording disqualifies Stage 6 compliance.

💰 Revenue Implications

→Higher severity_index → higher DRG weight → higher Medicare base payment
→Accurate acuity documentation protects against claim downcoding by payer

💸 Costs Incurred

→Nurse labor: triage assessment time (20–45 min per patient)
→Equipment: pulse oximeter, BP monitor, thermometer usage
→EHR entry labor cost per vital sign observation recorded
drg_weight ∝ f(severity_index, comorbidity_count)
Higher severity_index → higher drg_weight → higher allowed_amount_usd from Medicare/payer

A physician examines the patient and assigns the primary ICD-10-CM diagnosis code — the single most important clinical-financial link in the entire encounter. This triggers DRG assignment, determines HEDIS measure eligibility, and is the primary FWA risk point (upcoding occurs here). ICD-10-CM (FY2025)

Primary diagnosis code (icd10_code) must be the most specific code available. E.g., E11.65 not E11.9. Code specificity directly determines DRG assignment and reimbursement level.

LOINC — Laboratory Observations

hba1c_pct → LOINC 4548-4; glucose_mg_dl → LOINC 2345-7; creatinine → LOINC 2160-0; WBC → LOINC 6690-2; NT-proBNP → LOINC 33762-6.

RxNorm — Medication Coding

All prescribed medications use RxNorm CUI codes. Enables PDC (Proportion of Days Covered) calculation for HEDIS medication adherence measures. Metformin = RxCUI 860975.

FHIR R4 — Condition Resource

Condition.code (ICD-10), Condition.clinicalStatus, Condition.verificationStatus. MedicationRequest.medication (RxNorm). DiagnosticReport groups lab Observations.

HEDIS 2025 — CDC Measure

Comprehensive Diabetes Care (CDC): HbA1c testing (hedis_hba1c_tested) and poor control flag (hedis_hba1c_poor_control) are HEDIS numerator/denominator elements directly generated in this phase.

DRG — Diagnosis Related Groups

ICD-10 principal diagnosis + procedure (CPT) + comorbidity_count → DRG assignment → drg_weight → Medicare base payment rate. Higher drg_weight = higher reimbursement.

💰 Revenue Determined Here

→DRG weight established: drives the entire Medicare payment amount
→Lab charges: each LOINC-coded test generates a CPT billable service
→Accurate diagnosis coding = accurate reimbursement (no revenue leakage)

⚠️ FWA Risk — Upcoding

→fraud_upcoding_flag: ~4% base rate; inflates submitted_charge by 1.4–2.2×
→OIG watches for: providers who bill highest E&M codes 100% of the time
→RAC audits recover overpayments; penalties up to 3× the false claim amount
drg_weight = f(icd10_code, cpt_code, comorbidity_count) + ε
Medicare_payment ≈ drg_weight × hospital_base_rate (varies by geographic region)
submitted_charge_usd *= 1.4–2.2 when fraud_upcoding_flag = 1

The patient enters the operating room for the surgical procedure. This is the highest-cost, highest-revenue phase of the encounter. Every minute of OR time has a direct financial and supply cost attached. The CPT code recorded here drives the surgical kit selection (supply chain demand), the professional fee, and the charge capture. OR efficiency metrics — turnover time, utilization rate, on-time start — are the primary operational KPIs in this phase.

CPT / HCPCS — Procedure Coding

Each surgical act maps to a specific CPT code. E.g., 27447 = Total Knee Arthroplasty; 47562 = Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy. CPT code determines kit selection, OR time baseline, and professional fee.

FHIR R4 — Procedure Resource

Procedure.code (CPT), Procedure.performedPeriod (or_start to or_end), Procedure.performer (surgeon NPI), Procedure.usedReference (surgical kit).

JCI 8th Ed — IPSG Goal 4

Safe Surgery Protocol: WHO Surgical Safety Checklist must be documented (Sign-In, Time-Out, Sign-Out). Correct site/procedure/patient verification before incision. Never-event prevention.

ISO 7101 — Surgical Quality

Surgical site infection (SSI) rates are a core ISO 7101 quality metric. If SSI occurs, incident is flagged (safety_incident_flag) and PDSA cycle initiated (pdsa_cycle_id).

HIMSS EMRAM Stage 5+

Electronic medication administration record (eMAR) in OR and CPOE for all surgical orders required. Stage 6 adds closed-loop medication verification in the OR setting.

💰 Revenue Generated

→Surgeon professional fee (CPT-based, billed separately via npi_billing)
→Facility fee: OR room time × per-minute rate (~$50–$100/min)
→Anesthesia professional fee (billed per 15-min units)
→Implant/device pass-through charges (e.g., joint prosthesis at cost + markup)

💸 Costs Incurred

→Surgical kit cost (kit_unit_cost_usd) — highest supply spend per encounter
→OR nursing team labor (multi-staff, 8–12 hr shifts)
→OR facility overhead: sterilization, HVAC, equipment depreciation
→Inventory holding cost for surgical kit safety stock
submitted_charge_usd = (actual_or_minutes × or_rate_per_min) + kit_unit_cost_usd + facility_per_diem + anesthesia_units × unit_rate
When fraud_upcoding_flag = 1: submitted_charge_usd × [1.4, 2.2]
days_of_supply = kit_current_stock / (weekly_procedure_volume / 7)

JCI 8th Ed — IPSG Goals 3, 5, 6

IPSG 3: Safe medication management. IPSG 5: Hand hygiene compliance (HAI prevention). IPSG 6: Fall risk assessment and prevention protocol. All generate operational data.

ISO 7101 — PDSA in Action

When safety_incident_flag = 1, pdsa_cycle_id is created. DO phase = implementing the improvement (e.g., new hand hygiene protocol). Root cause analysis follows within 72 hours.

CAHPS / HCAHPS Survey

All 7 CAHPS dimensions in the dataset are formed during inpatient nursing care: nurse communication, doctor communication, responsiveness, pain management, discharge info, cleanliness, and quietness.

HIMSS EMRAM Stage 6–7

himss_emram_stage measures digital maturity. Stage 7 requires closed-loop medication administration (eMAR at bedside), real-time nursing documentation, and HIE participation — all reducing safety incidents.

HEDIS — Care Gap Management

During inpatient stay, open care gaps (e.g., hedis_hba1c_poor_control) trigger clinical intervention. Post-discharge follow-up compliance also begins to be planned in this phase.

CMS — HACRP & HRRP

Hospital-Acquired Condition Reduction Program: CMS penalizes hospitals in the lowest performance quartile for HAIs. safety_incident_flag and incident_type directly feed HACRP reporting.

💰 Revenue Accruing

→Daily facility per-diem charge × los_days
→Daily nursing care charge (per-hour rate × nurse_fte)
→Ancillary charges: physical therapy, respiratory therapy, daily labs

💸 Costs Incurred

→Nursing labor (largest single cost: ~$75–$110/hour RN fully loaded)
→Overtime premium: 1.5× base rate when overtime_hours > threshold
→HAI treatment cost: MRSA adds $40,000–$60,000 per incident
→CMS HAC penalty: 1% Medicare payment reduction for lowest quartile
→Consumable supply cost per bed-day (IV bags, catheters, bandages)
Nursing_labor_cost = shift_hours × nurse_hourly_rate × nurse_fte × [1.5 if overtime]
HAI_financial_impact = P(safety_incident_flag=1|burnout,ratio) × avg_HAI_treatment_cost
P(safety_incident_flag) ↑ as patients_per_nurse_ratio ↑ AND burnout_exhaustion_mbi ↑

FHIR R4 — Claim & ClaimResponse

Claim resource contains all billing elements. ClaimResponse (payer response) contains adjudication details: allowed amount, contractual adjustments, denial codes (mapped as adjudication.reason).

CMS — CARC Denial Reason Codes

Standard Claim Adjustment Reason Codes: CO-4 (service not covered), CO-16 (missing information), CO-18 (duplicate), CO-50 (not medically necessary), CO-197 (pre-auth required), CO-11 (diagnosis inconsistent). Dataset includes denial_reason_code.

DRG — Prospective Payment System

Medicare pays hospitals a fixed amount based on drg_weight × base_rate, regardless of actual submitted_charge. Understanding DRG payment vs charge is key to margin analysis.

HIPAA — 837P/837I Transaction Standards

Electronic claim submission must follow HIPAA 5010 transaction standards (837I for institutional, 837P for professional claims). Non-compliant formats cause automatic rejection.

JCI — Documentation Standards

Claim denials traced to missing JCI-required documentation (incomplete discharge summary, missing consent) create a feedback loop from Financial back to Clinical Quality.

💰 Cash Inflow

→allowed_amount_usd received from payer upon claim approval
→patient_responsibility_usd collected via billing statement or at point of care
→Successful appeals of denied claims recover denied revenue

💸 Revenue Leakage

→Contractual adjustment: submitted_charge minus allowed_amount (40–60% of chargemaster)
→Denied claims: cost of rework + potential write-off if appeal fails
→Bad debt: unrecovered patient_responsibility_usd from self-pay patients
→RAC audit repayments + False Claims Act penalties (FWA exposure)

The 30-day window after discharge is the highest-risk period for readmission. Hospitals with proactive follow-up programs — phone calls within 48 hours, medication reconciliation checks, telehealth check-ins — significantly reduce readmission rates. If the patient is readmitted within 30 days, the encounter lifecycle restarts from Phase 2, but the financial implications are different: CMS may not reimburse for the readmission and may penalize the hospital under HRRP. This phase closes the clinical quality loop and feeds the PDSA STUDY phase.

CMS — HRRP Penalties

Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program: excess 30-day readmissions for AMI, CHF, Pneumonia, COPD, Hip/Knee arthroplasty, and CABG result in up to 3% Medicare payment reduction across ALL Medicare cases (not just readmissions).

HEDIS 2025 — Care Continuity

HEDIS Transitions of Care measure: % of patients with a follow-up visit within 7 days of discharge. hedis_hba1c_poor_control flags care gaps requiring outreach. NCQA uses these for health plan ratings.

ISO 7101 — PDSA STUDY & ACT

pdsa_cycle_id closes here. STUDY: compare readmission rates before/after quality intervention. ACT: if improvement confirmed, scale protocol hospital-wide. If not, revise and restart PLAN phase.

FHIR R4 — CarePlan Resource

CarePlan.activity (follow-up visits planned), CarePlan.goal (reduce readmission risk). If patient is readmitted, a new Encounter resource is created linked to the original patient_id, enabling longitudinal tracking.

HIMSS EMRAM Stage 7 — HIE

Stage 7 requires Health Information Exchange (HIE) participation. Discharge summary flows electronically to PCP and community providers. himss_emram_stage covariate shows its impact on readmission reduction.

→Outpatient follow-up visit billing (lower revenue, but preferred)

💸 Readmission Costs & Penalties

→HRRP penalty: up to 3% reduction on all Medicare payments (can = $1M–$10M annually for large hospital)
→CMS readmission claim non-payment: hospital absorbs full cost of readmission care
→Care coordination program cost: ~$200–$500 per patient for 30-day follow-up program
→Readmission average cost: $15,000–$25,000 per inpatient episode (borne by hospital)
HRRP_penalty = f(readmission_30d_flag rate − national_benchmark) × total_Medicare_revenue × max_penalty_rate (3%)
ROI_of_follow_up_program = (readmissions_prevented × avg_readmission_cost) − program_cost
Typical ROI: $15,000 saved per prevented readmission vs $500 follow-up cost → 30:1 return

🏥 Clinical Analytics — Key Concepts

Core Clinical Metrics

  • HbA1c (Haemoglobin A1c)
    A blood test measuring average blood sugar over 3 months. Normal <5.7%; Pre-diabetic 5.7–6.4%; Diabetic ≥6.5%. Key HEDIS measure for Diabetes Management.
  • SpO2 (Oxygen Saturation)
    Percentage of haemoglobin carrying oxygen. Normal: 95–100%. Below 90% = medical emergency.
  • BMI (Body Mass Index)
    Weight (kg) / Height² (m²). Underweight <18.5, Normal 18.5–24.9, Overweight 25–29.9, Obese ≥30.
  • Blood Pressure (BP)
    Systolic/Diastolic (e.g., 120/80 mmHg). Hypertension: systolic ≥130 or diastolic ≥80.
  • PDC (Proportion of Days Covered)
    Medication adherence metric: days medication available / days in period. PDC ≥80% = adherent. Key HEDIS measure.
  • LOS (Length of Stay)
    Number of days a patient stays in hospital. High LOS consumes resources and is a proxy for case complexity.

Readmission & Safety

  • 30-Day Readmission
    When a patient is re-hospitalised within 30 days of discharge. CMS penalises hospitals financially for excess readmissions (HRRP programme). Target: <15%.
  • HAI (Hospital-Acquired Infection)
    An infection the patient did not have on admission — contracted inside the hospital (e.g., MRSA, C. difficile, Catheter-associated UTI). Target: approaching 0 per 1,000 patient days.
  • Sepsis
    A life-threatening immune overreaction to infection. Leading cause of death and a major focus of quality improvement.
  • Mortality Rate (In-hospital)
    Percentage of admitted patients who die during the encounter. Risk-adjusted mortality used for benchmarking.
  • ALOS / Case Mix Index (CMI)
    ALOS = average LOS (benchmark 4–5 days for general medicine). CMI = average clinical complexity. Higher CMI = sicker patients = higher expected resource use and cost.

Acuity & Triage

  • Acuity
    How sick or medically complex a patient is. High-acuity patients need more intensive care.
  • ESI (Emergency Severity Index)
    5-level triage scale. ESI-1 = immediately life-threatening; ESI-5 = non-urgent. Drives ER workflow and staffing.
  • Severity Index
    Composite numeric score (e.g., 0–1) reflecting patient complexity, derived from diagnosis codes and comorbidity counts.
  • Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI)
    Validated scoring system predicting 10-year mortality based on comorbid conditions. Used in risk adjustment.

Post-Discharge & Care Coordination

  • Discharge Disposition
    Where the patient goes after leaving: Home, Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF), Rehab, Hospice, etc.
  • Post-Discharge Follow-Up
    Recommended follow-up within 7–14 days of discharge, especially for heart failure and pneumonia patients.
  • Care Gap
    A missed or overdue recommended service for a patient with a known chronic condition (e.g., diabetic without HbA1c test in 12 months).
  • Telehealth
    Delivery of healthcare via phone, video call, or digital app without in-person visit.

⚙ Operational Analytics — Key Concepts

Patient Flow & Bed Management

  • Census
    Number of inpatients currently occupying beds at any given time. A daily census is a snapshot of hospital occupancy.
  • Bed Occupancy Rate
    % of beds currently in use. Target: 75–85%. Above 90% strains staff; below 70% wastes capacity.
  • Bed Turnaround Time
    Time between a patient vacating a bed and a new patient being admitted. Involves cleaning, inspection, and setup.
  • ADT System
    Admission, Discharge, Transfer — tracks patient location movements within the hospital in real time.
  • PACU / ICU
    PACU = Post-Anaesthesia Care Unit (recovery room). ICU = Intensive Care Unit for critically ill patients (nurse ratio typically 1:2).
  • Boarding
    When an ER patient cannot be moved to an inpatient bed because none are available. A major throughput problem.

ER Throughput & Wait Times

  • LWBS (Left Without Being Seen)
    % of ER patients who leave before being seen by a physician. Key patient experience and safety metric. Target: <2%.
  • Door-to-Doctor Time
    Time from patient arrival at ER to first physician assessment. Target: <30 minutes.
  • EDLOS (Emergency Department LOS)
    Total time in ED — from arrival to admission or discharge. Target: <4 hours for non-admitted patients.
  • Diversion
    When the ER is so overcrowded that incoming ambulances are redirected to another hospital.

Operating Room (OR) Efficiency

  • OR Utilisation Rate
    % of scheduled OR time actually used for procedures. Target: 70–85%.
  • OR Turnover Time
    Minutes between one surgery ending and the next beginning. Includes cleaning, setup, and patient positioning. Target: <25 minutes.
  • First Case On-Time Start
    % of first surgical cases that begin on schedule. A leading indicator of OR efficiency.
  • Cancellation Rate
    % of scheduled surgeries cancelled on the day of the procedure.

Quality & Safety Incidents

  • Incident Report
    Formal documented record of an adverse event (patient fall, medication error, equipment failure).
  • Never Events
    Serious, preventable adverse events that should never occur (e.g., surgery on wrong body part). CMS does not reimburse.
  • Near Miss
    An event that could have caused harm but did not. Valuable learning opportunities for quality improvement.
  • PSI (Patient Safety Indicators)
    AHRQ-developed quality measures. PSI-90 = Composite Safety Index covering hospital complications.
  • Staff-to-Patient Ratio
    Patients per nurse or physician. Critical HR-Operations cross-domain metric: lower ratios = safer care.

💰 Financial Analytics & Revenue Cycle — Key Concepts

The Revenue Cycle (7 Stages)

  • 1. Charge Capture
    Every service delivered is assigned a CPT/HCPCS code and a dollar amount. This is the "submitted charge."
  • 2. Coding & Documentation
    Medical coders ensure diagnosis (ICD-10) and procedure (CPT) codes are accurate. Errors generate FWA risk flags.
  • 3. Claims Submission
    A claim is submitted electronically to the patient's insurer (payer) with claim_id, NPI, and payer_id.
  • 4. Adjudication
    The payer reviews and decides: Pay (allowed amount), Deny, or Request more information.
  • 5. Payment & Remittance
    Payer sends an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) and payment. Difference = "contractual adjustment."
  • 6. A/R Management
    Unpaid claims are tracked and followed up. Days in AR target: <40 days.
  • 7. Patient Collections
    Remaining balance after insurer payment is billed to the patient (co-pay, deductible, self-pay).

Key Financial Terms

  • Net Patient Revenue (NPR)
    Total revenue actually collected from patients and payers after subtracting adjustments and denials.
  • Contractual Adjustment
    Difference between hospital's list price (chargemaster) and negotiated rate with insurer. Typically 40–60% of list price.
  • Chargemaster (CDM)
    Hospital's official list of prices for every service, drug, and supply. Published but rarely what anyone actually pays.
  • Cost-to-Charge Ratio (CCR)
    Converts billed charges to estimated actual costs. Used in financial modelling.
  • Denial Rate & Reason Codes
    % claims denied. Target: <5%. Codes: CO-4 = not covered; CO-16 = missing info; CO-96 = non-covered charge.
  • DRG (Diagnosis-Related Group)
    Classification grouping inpatient stays by diagnosis/procedure for Medicare reimbursement. Fixed payment per group.

Fraud, Waste & Abuse (FWA)

  • Upcoding
    Billing for a more expensive service than provided (e.g., billing 99215 when only 99212 occurred). Flagged by fraud analytics.
  • Unbundling
    Billing separately for services that should be packaged together — inflating the total claim.
  • Phantom Billing
    Billing for services never provided. CMS and OIG actively investigate.
  • RAC Audit
    Recovery Audit Contractor — CMS programme reviewing claims and demanding repayment for improper billing.
  • Statistical Anomaly
    Unusually high billing pattern (e.g., physician billing 99215 100% of the time). Triggers FWA investigation.

Equipment Utilisation

  • Equipment Utilisation Rate
    usage_minutes / available_minutes × 100%. Target: 70–85%. Low = wasted capital; high = potential bottleneck.

👥 Human Resources Analytics — Key Concepts

Workforce Structure

  • FTE (Full-Time Equivalent)
    Staffing volume measure. One FTE = one full-time employee (40 hrs/week). Part-timer at 20 hrs = 0.5 FTE.
  • Nurse-to-Patient Ratio
    Patients each nurse is responsible for. California mandates max 1:5 in med-surg; 1:2 in ICU.
  • Span of Control
    Number of direct reports a manager oversees. Standard healthcare management: 8–12 direct reports.
  • Per Diem Staff
    Nurses hired day-by-day to cover shortages. More expensive but provides flexibility.
  • Agency Staff
    Staff hired through a staffing agency. Very expensive; associated with quality risks due to unfamiliarity.
  • Tenure
    Length of time an employee has worked for the organisation. Longer tenure correlates with lower error rates and better outcomes.

Burnout — The MBI Framework

  • Emotional Exhaustion (EE)
    Feeling emotionally drained and depleted. The primary driver of burnout. High EE + high overtime = high turnover risk.
  • Depersonalisation (DP)
    Developing a detached, cynical attitude toward patients. A defence mechanism. High DP + low satisfaction = medication error risk.
  • Personal Accomplishment (PA)
    Sense of competence and meaningful achievement. Low PA = reduced burnout resilience. Low PA + high shift intensity = 6-month turnover predictor.
  • MBI Composite Score
    A composite score derived from the three dimensions. Thresholds define Low / Moderate / High burnout risk.

Turnover & Retention

  • Voluntary Turnover Rate
    % who choose to leave. Benchmark: 15–25% annually for nurses. Each departure costs ~$50,000–$90,000 to replace.
  • Turnover Risk Index
    Predictive score combining burnout, tenure, overtime, and satisfaction to estimate probability of leaving in 90 days.
  • RNAP (Registered Nurse Available Pool)
    Local labour market supply of available nurses. Tight RNAP = higher wages needed to recruit.
  • Exit Interview & Succession Planning
    Structured conversation with departing employees. Succession planning identifies internal candidates for future leadership.

Scheduling & Shift Analytics

  • Shift Intensity
    Numeric measure of how demanding a shift was — based on patient acuity, census, and incident count.
  • Overtime Hours
    Hours beyond 40-hour week. Chronic overtime is a leading predictor of burnout and medication errors. Target: <10 hrs/month.
  • On-Call
    Periods when a staff member must be available to come in if needed, even when not formally scheduled.
  • Float Pool
    Cross-trained nurses assignable to any unit based on demand. Improves staffing flexibility.
  • HPPD (Hours Per Patient Day)
    Total nursing hours / total patient days. A key operational-HR cross-domain metric.

📦 Supply Chain Analytics — Key Concepts

Inventory Management Fundamentals

  • Par Level
    Minimum quantity that should always be in stock. When stock falls below par, automatic reorder is triggered.
  • Reorder Point
    Inventory level at which a new order should be placed, accounting for lead time. E.g., usage 10/day + lead time 5 days = reorder at 50 units.
  • Safety Stock
    Extra inventory held as buffer against supply chain disruptions or demand spikes.
  • Lead Time
    Days between placing an order and receiving goods. High variability requires more safety stock.
  • Unit Cost & SKU
    Cost per individual item, tracked per Stock Keeping Unit (unique inventory identifier).
  • EOQ (Economic Order Quantity)
    Mathematically optimal order quantity minimising combined ordering and holding costs.

Healthcare-Specific Supply Terms

  • Formulary
    Official list of drugs stocked and approved for use. Items not on the formulary require special approval.
  • Consignment Inventory
    Supplies owned by the vendor and stored at the hospital — hospital pays only when items are used.
  • Surgical Kit
    Pre-packaged set of supplies for a specific procedure (e.g., hip replacement). Cost tracked per kit.
  • Consumable Supplies
    Single-use items consumed per admission: IV bags, syringes, catheters, bandages, gloves.
  • Expiration Date Management
    Critical for medications and sterile supplies. Expired inventory must be wasted.
  • Shrinkage / Waste
    Inventory losses due to expiration, damage, theft, or overuse. Target: <2% of total inventory value.

Procurement Analytics

  • GPO (Group Purchasing Organisation)
    Consortium of hospitals negotiating collective purchasing contracts for volume discounts.
  • Vendor Scorecard
    Performance rating for each supplier: on-time delivery rate, defect rate, and pricing accuracy.
  • Backorder
    When a supplier cannot fulfil an order. In healthcare, drug backorders can directly harm patients.
  • Substitution Rate
    % of orders fulfilled with an equivalent substitute when the primary item is unavailable.
  • Supply Cost per Admission
    Total supply cost / number of admissions. Key financial-supply chain KPI. Benchmark: 20–30% of NPR.
Why this matters for data analytics: Every regulation and standard listed below defines specific data requirements, reporting obligations, or quality benchmarks that directly shape what data hospitals collect, how they structure it, and what KPIs they must track. Understanding these frameworks is essential for building a meaningful analytics platform.
Privacy & Compliance

HIPAA

▼
Health Insurance Portability & Accountability Act (1996) — the single most important US healthcare privacy law.
Purpose
Establishes national standards for protecting sensitive patient health information (PHI). Every hospital, insurer, and data analyst working with patient data must comply. HIPAA says any information that can identify a patient AND relates to their health condition, treatment, or payment is "Protected Health Information" and must be kept private and secure.
Three Core Rules
  • Privacy Rule — Controls who can access and use PHI. Gives patients rights over their own health information.
  • Security Rule — Sets technical and physical safeguards to protect electronic PHI (ePHI).
  • Breach Notification Rule — Requires notification of affected individuals, HHS, and sometimes media if unsecured PHI is breached.
Healthcare Business Context
Data Analytics Patient Privacy De-identification Breach Detection

For analytics teams, HIPAA compliance means working with de-identified data. The Safe Harbor method removes 18 specific identifiers (names, SSN, dates except year, geographic data below state level, etc.). In synthetic datasets, dates are stored as year-only, geography at state level, and all person-linked identifiers replaced with synthetic IDs. Ages over 89 are grouped (a HIPAA-specific rule). Understanding de-identification is critical before any analytics work begins.

CMS

▼
Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — the US federal agency administering Medicare and Medicaid.
Purpose
Sets billing codes, reimbursement rates, and quality requirements for US hospitals. Mandates FHIR adoption for data exchange. Operates the HRRP (Hospital Readmissions Reduction Programme) that financially penalises hospitals for excess readmissions. The most impactful federal agency for hospital revenue.
Healthcare Business Context
Reimbursement Quality Standards FHIR Mandate HRRP Penalties

CMS determines how much hospitals get paid (through DRG payments, Medicare rates), what quality measures they must report (HEDIS), and what technology standards they must adopt (FHIR). Analytics platforms must align with CMS requirements to be useful. CMS also operates RAC audits that review hospital claims for improper billing.

The Joint Commission (TJC)

▼
Primary US hospital accreditation body. Accreditation is required to receive Medicare/Medicaid funding.
Purpose
Conducts compliance audits (surveys) of hospitals covering patient safety, clinical documentation, medication management, and quality improvement. Accreditation is effectively mandatory for any hospital accepting Medicare/Medicaid patients. A TJC survey is like a comprehensive audit.
Healthcare Business Context
Accreditation Patient Safety Documentation Standards Medicare Eligibility

TJC requirements drive many data fields in hospital systems: completeness scores, medication error flags, and quality initiative tracking all exist partly to satisfy TJC standards. Analytics dashboards often include TJC compliance metrics.

OIG

▼
Office of Inspector General — enforcement arm for healthcare fraud, waste, and abuse (FWA).
Purpose
Investigates healthcare fraud, waste, and abuse. Issues exclusion lists of banned providers. Works with CMS to audit hospital billing practices. OIG checks NPIs for excluded/sanctioned providers. The primary enforcement body ensuring billing integrity.
Healthcare Business Context
Fraud Detection FWA Enforcement Provider Exclusion RAC Audits

FWA analytics is a major use case for the platform. It must detect upcoding, unbundling, phantom billing, and statistical anomalies in provider billing patterns. OIG exclusion lists must be cross-referenced with NPI numbers in the dataset to ensure no sanctioned providers are active.

AHRQ

▼
Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality — federal research agency producing quality and safety standards.
Purpose
Produces quality and safety standards referenced in analytics, including the Patient Safety Indicators (PSI) — a set of measures identifying potential hospital complications. PSI-90 is the Composite Safety Index. Also develops CAHPS survey methodology for patient experience measurement.
Healthcare Business Context
Safety Indicators PSI-90 Benchmarking Quality Research CAHPS Surveys

PSI scores derived from AHRQ standards are key operational and clinical KPIs. They measure hospital-acquired infection rates, surgical complication rates, and pressure ulcer incidence. Analytics platforms track PSIs for quality improvement and benchmarking against national standards.

FDA

▼
Food & Drug Administration — regulates drugs, devices, and clinical trials.
Purpose
Regulates medications, medical devices, and clinical trials. Ensures drug safety and efficacy before market approval. Issues recalls and safety communications for marketed products.
Healthcare Business Context
Drug Safety Device Regulation Formulary Management Recall Tracking

FDA approvals and recalls affect supply chain data (formulary changes, backorders). Medication error tracking in the analytics platform must consider FDA-regulated drug classifications and safety communications. New drug approvals may shift formulary decisions and impact cost analytics.
Data Interoperability Standards

HL7 FHIR

▼
Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources — the modern gold standard for electronic health data exchange.
Purpose
Created by HL7 International and now mandated by CMS for US healthcare providers. FHIR organises every piece of clinical data into "Resources" — standardised data objects with defined schemas mapping to real-world clinical concepts. FHIR Bundles group multiple Resources into a single JSON document. R4 (2019) is the current widely-deployed standard; R5 (2023) adds enhancements.
Key Resources for Analytics
  • Patient — Core entity; every encounter links back to this
  • Encounter — A single hospital visit, ER trip, or appointment
  • Observation — Lab results, vital signs (HbA1c, BP, SpO2)
  • Condition — Diagnoses linked to ICD-10 codes
  • Procedure — Surgeries, therapies linked to CPT codes
  • MedicationRequest — Prescriptions linked to RxNorm codes
  • Claim — Billing submission to a payer
  • CarePlan — Treatment goals supporting HEDIS care gap tracking
Healthcare Business Context
Data Exchange EHR Integration CMS Mandate JSON/REST API

If healthcare data were packages being shipped between hospitals, labs, and pharmacies — FHIR is the standardised packaging, labelling, and addressing system ensuring every package arrives correctly. Analytics platforms built on FHIR-compatible data can integrate with any modern EHR system seamlessly.

ICD-10-CM

▼
International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, Clinical Modification — diagnosis coding.
Purpose
The standardised system for coding every known disease, condition, injury, and symptom. Think of it as a universal product barcode for diseases. Instead of writing "Type 2 Diabetes with kidney complications," a doctor writes "E11.65" — a precise, machine-readable code understood worldwide.
Code Structure
  • Category (1 letter) — Broad disease group. E.g., 'E' = Endocrine diseases
  • Etiology (2 digits) — Specific disease. E.g., 'E11' = Type 2 Diabetes
  • Clinical detail (2–3 chars) — E.g., 'E11.65' = T2DM with hyperglycaemia
Common codes: E11.x (Type 2 Diabetes), I10 (Hypertension), J18.9 (Pneumonia), Z79.4 (Long-term insulin use), F32.x (Depressive disorders).
Healthcare Business Context
Diagnosis Coding Billing Trigger DRG Classification Disease Analytics

ICD-10 codes are the foundation of clinical analytics. They determine DRG assignments (and thus reimbursement), enable population health analysis, and feed quality measures like HEDIS. Every encounter must have accurate ICD-10 codes for both clinical and financial reasons.

CPT / HCPCS

▼
Procedure coding systems — CPT for medical/surgical services; HCPCS for supplies and equipment.
Purpose
While ICD-10-CM codes what is wrong with the patient, CPT and HCPCS codes describe what was done to treat them. CPT (maintained by AMA) provides 5-digit codes for medical, surgical, and diagnostic services (e.g., 93000 = ECG). HCPCS Level II extends CPT with alphanumeric codes for supplies, equipment, and non-physician services (e.g., A4253 = blood glucose test strips), required for Medicare billing.
Healthcare Business Context
Procedure Billing Medicare Required Charge Capture FWA Detection

CPT codes are the basis for the "charge capture" stage of revenue cycle management. Every coded procedure generates a billable claim. FWA analytics monitors for upcoding (billing higher CPT codes than warranted) and unbundling (splitting bundled services).

LOINC

▼
Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes — universal standard for lab tests and observations.
Purpose
A universal standard for identifying laboratory tests, clinical measurements, and survey responses. Without LOINC, "HbA1c" might be called "Glycated Haemoglobin," "A1C," "GHb," or "HbA1c" depending on the lab — making cross-system analytics impossible. LOINC standardises the name across all systems.
Examples
  • 4548-4 — Haemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), the blood sugar control test for diabetics
  • 8480-6 — Systolic blood pressure
  • 2823-3 — Potassium level in serum
Healthcare Business Context
Lab Standardisation Cross-System Analytics HEDIS Measures

LOINC codes enable analytics platforms to aggregate lab results across multiple facilities and EHR systems. Critical for HEDIS measures like diabetes management (HbA1c testing rates) and hypertension control.

RxNorm

▼
NIH's standard vocabulary for clinical drugs and medications.
Purpose
Normalises the many names a drug might have (brand name, generic name, manufacturer variant) into a single concept code (RxCUI). E.g., RxCUI 860975 = Metformin 500mg oral tablet. Links brand names (Glucophage) to generic equivalents (Metformin) for substitution analysis.
Healthcare Business Context
Drug Standardisation Formulary Analytics Medication Adherence PDC Tracking

RxNorm enables medication adherence analytics (PDC scores), brand-vs-generic cost analysis, and formulary compliance tracking. It is essential for HEDIS medication adherence measures and for detecting medication-related safety issues.
Quality & Accreditation Frameworks

HEDIS 2025

▼
Healthcare Effectiveness Data and Information Set — standardised performance measures by NCQA.
Purpose
Published annually by NCQA (National Committee for Quality Assurance). A set of standardised performance measures used by health plans and hospitals to report on quality of care. Think of HEDIS as a report card for healthcare providers. Insurers use HEDIS scores to decide which hospital networks to include, and CMS uses them to adjust Medicare reimbursements. Low HEDIS scores cost hospitals money.
Key Measure Categories
  • Effectiveness of Care — Did patients receive recommended treatments? (e.g., HbA1c testing for diabetics)
  • Access/Availability of Care — Could patients get appointments when needed?
  • Experience of Care — Patient satisfaction scores (sourced from CAHPS surveys)
  • Utilisation & Risk — ER visit rates, hospital admission rates for chronic conditions
Care Gap Alerts
A "care gap" is when a patient is overdue for a recommended service. Analytics platforms flag these for proactive outreach: HbA1c testing, blood pressure control, retinal eye exams for diabetics, statin therapy for cardiovascular patients, colorectal cancer screening (ages 45–75).
Healthcare Business Context
Quality Report Card Reimbursement Impact Care Gap Analytics Network Inclusion

HEDIS is the most commercially impactful quality framework. Analytics platforms must track HEDIS measures across clinical, operational, and financial domains. HEDIS scores directly influence hospital revenue through CMS reimbursement adjustments and insurer network decisions.

CAHPS

▼
Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems — standardised patient experience survey.
Purpose
Developed by AHRQ, CAHPS measures patients' experiences with their healthcare — not clinical outcomes, but how patients felt about their care. Results feed directly into HEDIS "Experience of Care" measures.
Key Survey Domains
  • Communication with Doctors — Did the doctor explain things clearly? Listen carefully? Treat with respect?
  • Communication with Nurses — Did nurses explain medications? Respond promptly to call buttons?
  • Responsiveness of Staff — How quickly did staff respond to requests for help?
  • Care Transitions — Clear discharge instructions? Side effects explained?
  • Hospital Rating (0–10) — Overall satisfaction score. Key HEDIS input.
  • Willingness to Recommend — Would the patient recommend this hospital to family/friends?
Healthcare Business Context
Patient Experience HEDIS Input Hospital Ratings Operational Feedback

CAHPS scores map directly to data columns like communication_score, responsiveness_score, and patient_satisfaction_score. Typically sourced from post-discharge phone surveys. Poor CAHPS scores indicate operational problems that analytics can help diagnose and resolve.

ISO 7101

▼
Healthcare Quality Management Systems (2023) — first international ISO standard for healthcare QMS.
Purpose
Published in 2023, ISO 7101 is the first international ISO standard specifically for healthcare quality management systems. It provides a framework for hospitals to systematically improve quality and safety using data-driven methodologies.
PDSA Cycle (Core Methodology)
  • PLAN — Identify a problem (e.g., high HAI rate). Develop hypothesis and plan a small test.
  • DO — Implement the test on a small scale (e.g., new hand hygiene protocol on one ward).
  • STUDY — Analyse results. Did the HAI rate drop? Calculate effect size.
  • ACT — If successful, scale to the whole hospital. If not, revise and re-test.
Healthcare Business Context
Quality Improvement PDSA Methodology International Standard Data-Driven Improvement

ISO 7101 mandates data-driven quality improvement. Analytics platforms support this by tracking PDSA cycles, logging interventions and outcomes (intervention_date, affected unit_id, outcome_metric), and enabling before/after comparisons. Quality logs with pdsa_phase and quality_initiative_id fields support this framework.

JCI 8th Edition

▼
Joint Commission International — global gold standard for hospital accreditation.
Purpose
The international arm of The Joint Commission. Hospitals in 100+ countries pursue JCI accreditation to signal excellence in patient safety and quality. The 8th edition is the latest standard covering all aspects of hospital operations.
Key Requirements for Analytics
  • Patient Safety Goals (IPSGs) — Six international goals: correct patient ID, safe surgery, infection control. Direct analytic KPIs.
  • Medical Records Standards — Complete, accurate, timely documentation driving completeness scores.
  • Quality Improvement Chapter — Requires hospitals to use data to drive improvement — supports PDSA analytics.
  • Medication Management — Strict protocols for high-alert medications (anticoagulants, chemotherapy). Links to medication_error_flag.
Healthcare Business Context
Global Accreditation Patient Safety Medication Safety Data-Driven QI

JCI accreditation is a competitive differentiator, especially for hospitals serving international patients. Analytics platforms must track IPSG compliance, medication error rates, and documentation completeness to support JCI reporting. Training compliance rate (100% for mandatory training) is a JCI requirement.

HIMSS EMRAM / AMAM

▼
Maturity models measuring how advanced a hospital's digital health infrastructure is.
Purpose
HIMSS publishes maturity models measuring digital health advancement. EMRAM (Electronic Medical Record Adoption Model) is a 7-stage model (0–7) for inpatient settings. AMAM is the equivalent for outpatient/ambulatory settings. Stage 7 means fully paperless with data analytics, AI, and HIE capabilities.
Key Stages
  • Stage 0 — Paper-based records; no EMR
  • Stage 3 — Basic clinical documentation and CPOE (computerised physician order entry) in place
  • Stage 6 — Physician documentation, full medication management, and business intelligence
  • Stage 7 — Complete EMR; data analytics; AI-ready feature stores; participates in Health Information Exchange (HIE)
Healthcare Business Context
Digital Maturity EMR Adoption AI Readiness HIE Participation

HIMSS maturity levels determine what kind of analytics is feasible. A Stage 7-ready dataset contains structured, machine-readable data across all domains that can feed an AI analytics platform. This is a key selling point for analytics platform vendors targeting progressive hospitals.

ER Door-to-Doctor Time Minutes from patient arrival to first physician assessment. Target: <30 minutes. Calculated from triage_timestamp to physician_assessment_timestamp. Long waits increase LWBS rates and patient safety risk.
Bed Occupancy Rate % of licensed beds occupied. Target: 75–85%. Above 90% strains staff and causes ER boarding; below 70% wastes capacity and revenue.
OR Utilisation Rate % of scheduled OR time used productively. Target: 70–85%. OR time is the most expensive hospital resource. Underutilisation = lost revenue; overutilisation = staff burnout.
OR Turnover Time Minutes between end of one case and start of next. Target: <25 minutes. Includes cleaning, setup, and patient positioning. Long turnover reduces surgical volume and revenue.
LWBS Rate % of ER patients who leave before being seen. Target: <2%. A key patient experience and safety metric. High LWBS indicates capacity problems and potential delayed care for sicker patients.
Patient Safety Incident Rate Incidents per 1,000 patient days. Tracks never events, near misses, and patient falls. Key JCI and AHRQ PSI metric. Directly impacts accreditation status.
Appointment Lead Time Days from appointment request to appointment date. Target: <7 days for urgent; <21 days for routine. Long lead times drive ER usage for non-emergencies and reduce HEDIS access scores.
KPI NameWhat It MeasuresWhy It Matters
Net Revenue per Adjusted Admission NPR divided by case-mix adjusted admissions. Core revenue efficiency metric. Case-mix adjustment ensures fair comparison across hospitals with different patient complexity levels (CMI).
Claim Denial Rate % of claims denied on first submission. Target: <5%. Every denied claim requires rework, delaying payment. High denial rates indicate coding errors, missing documentation, or FWA risk.
Days in Accounts Receivable Average days to collect payment post-claim. Target: <40 days. Over 60 days signals billing problems. Directly impacts hospital cash flow and liquidity.
Cost per Adjusted Discharge Total operating costs per case-mix adjusted discharge. Benchmark varies by hospital type. Tracks operational cost efficiency. Combined with revenue metrics, determines margin per discharge.
Equipment Utilisation Rate Usage minutes / Available minutes. Target: 70–85% for major imaging equipment. MRI and CT scanners cost millions. Low utilisation = wasted capital; high = maintenance risk.
Supply Cost per Admission Total supply expense per inpatient admission. Trend monitoring for cost control. Includes consumable supplies and surgical kits. Benchmark: 20–30% of Net Patient Revenue.
FWA Detection Rate % of claims flagged by fraud analytics. Should match external audit findings within ±10%. Monitors for upcoding, unbundling, phantom billing, and statistical anomalies.
KPI NameWhat It MeasuresWhy It Matters
Nurse Turnover Rate % of nurses leaving annually. Target: <15%. Benchmark: US average ~22%. Each departure costs $50,000–$90,000 to replace. High turnover destabilises teams and increases error rates.
Burnout Prevalence Rate % of staff scoring 'High' on MBI. Alert threshold: >25% of any unit. Burned-out staff make more errors, leave faster, and produce worse outcomes. COVID-19 amplified this crisis globally.
Overtime Hours per FTE Average OT hours per full-time employee per quarter. Target: <10 hrs/month. Chronic overtime is a leading predictor of burnout and medication errors. Cross-domain HR-Operations metric.
Nurse-to-Patient Ratio Patients per nurse per shift. Benchmark: ≤5:1 general med-surg; ≤2:1 ICU. Mandated minimums vary by state. Lower ratios directly correlate with better patient outcomes and fewer errors.
Vacancy Rate % of budgeted positions currently unfilled. Target: <10%. High vacancy increases workload on remaining staff, creating a vicious cycle of burnout and more turnover.
Training Compliance Rate % of staff with current required training. JCI requirement: 100% for mandatory training. Non-compliance creates safety risks and can affect accreditation status during surveys.
KPI NameWhat It MeasuresWhy It Matters
Inventory Turnover Cost of goods used / Average inventory value. Target: 10–15x per year for hospitals. High turnover = efficient inventory management. Low turnover = excess stock tying up capital or expiration waste risk.
Stockout Rate # of times an item fell below par level. Target: approaching 0 for critical items. Stockouts of medications or surgical supplies can directly harm patients or cause procedure cancellations.
On-Time Delivery Rate % of orders delivered by promised date. Target: ≥98% for critical supplies. Late deliveries disrupt clinical operations and may force expensive emergency purchases or procedure delays.
Supply Cost as % of Revenue Total supply spend / Net patient revenue. Benchmark: 20–30% of NPR. Supply costs are the second-largest hospital expense after labour. Trend monitoring critical for cost control.
Expired Inventory Rate Value of expired goods / Total inventory value. Target: <0.5%. Expired medications and sterile supplies must be wasted. High rates indicate poor demand forecasting or over-ordering.
Lead Time Variance Standard deviation of actual vs. expected lead time. Low variance = reliable supply chain. High variance requires larger safety stock buffers, tying up capital and increasing carrying costs.
